# CleanupPostBorrowck
Model: deepseek-flash
Scope: historical-transformation-program-pair; concrete tested inputs, not a current rustc bug claim or universal proof.

## Elements Frequently Missed
- Matching only the exact old `BorrowKind::Fake` after the enum gains a `FakeBorrowKind` payload is too narrow; cleanup and promotion must match `BorrowKind::Fake(_)`.
- Do not treat deep fake borrows as ordinary promotable shared borrows merely because they look immutable or aliasable.
- A shallow fake borrow only protects the borrowed place itself, not its projections; deref patterns need a deep fake borrow to block guard mutation such as `*b = true`.
- The `SimplifyCfg` MIR-test hunks in the supplied patch mainly renumber locals and print `fake shallow`; they are not evidence of a separate `SimplifyCfg` semantic rewrite.
- Do not infer that a passing run-pass test alone establishes the fake-borrow cleanup invariant; explicitly inspect the resulting MIR and the guard-mutation diagnostic.
- Do not validate or filter MCDC conditions independently after the decision has already been queued. If any condition or the decision span is invalid, emit no `MCDCDecision`; downgrade all conditions to `MappingKind::Branch` instead.
- Empty mapping spans must be expanded to an adjacent `{` or `}` before coordinate conversion; otherwise zero-width regions can appear, as shown by old expectations `14:10 - 14:10`, `21:2 - 21:2`, and `14:39 - 14:39`.
- Incorrectly ordered or zero/misinterpreted coordinates can make `llvm-cov` fail; invalid regions should be dropped before codegen rather than relying on later filtering.
- The deferred path's per-mapping skip can remove one condition while retaining the decision mapping, violating the MCDC decision/condition relationship.
- After early `SourceRegion` construction, MIR dumps use numeric `line:col - line:col` formatting; after deferred raw `Span` storage, they may show path-bearing `Span` formatting such as `$DIR/file:line:col: line:col (#0)`.
- `CleanupPostBorrowck` coverage metadata is inherited from instrumentation, so changes to source-region construction affect both instrumentation dumps and later cleanup expectations; do not 'fix' cleanup independently.
- Do not emit a coverage region when validation fails: line/column must be nonzero, `end_col`'s high bit must be unset, and `(start_line, start_col) <= (end_line, end_col)`, or `llvm-cov` may exit fatally.
- A used function may legitimately have no regions after codegen-side discarding; record preparation must return `None` instead of retaining an old `bug!` assertion or emitting a zero-region record.
- Empty spans are not simply passed through: empty-span handling tries to expand to a neighboring `{` or `}`, but only within the function body span.
- The `BytePos(1)` adjustment is safe only because the code checks for the specific ASCII characters `{` and `}`; do not generalize it to arbitrary characters or multi-byte code points.
- Moving conversion to codegen changes MIR pretty-printing from numeric coordinates to `Span` text; expectations must be updated, and apparent coordinate changes such as `20:1 - 20:2` becoming `20:2 - 20:2` can reflect a stored empty span at MIR-dump time rather than final codegen coordinates.
- Doctest line numbers are adjusted in codegen, but columns are not (`FIXME(#79417)`).
- The regression cannot rely on ordinary source alone; the supplied test uses an auxiliary crate so the binary retains valid coverage metadata while the main crate's spans are deliberately discarded.
- Skipping the entire covfun record is intentional when the local-file-table check fails; do not merely delete the unused local file table entry, because the supplied historical choice is to skip the function.
- Indexing a bool vector sized to `covfun.virtual_file_mapping.local_file_table.len()` with `cov_span.file_id` requires each local file ID to remain a valid per-function index.
- When empty-span enlargement is moved into InstrumentCoverage, it occurs before `dedup_by`/`merge_if_eligible`, so previously separate mappings can merge; for example the async_closure `.cov-map` drops from two mappings to one.
- Codegen may replace enlargement with a debug assertion for empty spans; that assertion only fires in debug builds, so release behavior can silently discard spans and should not be treated as the validation mechanism.
- The empty check must include all region tables, e.g. via all coverage spans, or an empty table can be missed after refactoring.
- The test-only inject flag must be paired with an auxiliary instrumented crate; otherwise `llvm-cov` can complain if the test binary has no coverage metadata at all.
- Downstream `CleanupPostBorrowck` dumps are affected by earlier InstrumentCoverage span enlargement even though CleanupPostBorrowck's own logic did not change.
- Do not assume empty coverage spans are invalid during MIR instrumentation; under the late-enlargement ordering they are deliberately left empty until codegen coordinate generation.
- Enlarging empty spans in InstrumentCoverage changes observable MIR coverage statements and is not equivalent to enlarging them in codegen, even if the eventual line/column widening can look similar.
- `CleanupPostBorrowck` does not perform this enlargement; its empty-span expectations are downstream of InstrumentCoverage and must move with the instrumentation placement, not be fixed independently to non-empty spans.
- The codegen `debug_assert!(false, "can't make coords from empty span")` belongs to the early-enlargement/reverted ordering; restoring `ensure_non_empty_span` in map generation avoids that path.
- Changing `BytePos` by 1 is justified only after confirming the skipped byte is ASCII `{` or `}` at the appropriate adjacent position; `span_to_source` failure drops the empty span.
- Early enlargement before merge can collapse mappings: the async-closure example changes from two file-0 mappings to one and raw bytes shrink from 14 to 9, so mapping-count expectations are part of the behavior.

## Patterns Not Well Handled

### Erase every fake-borrow variant in post-borrowck cleanup, using deep fake borrows for deref-pattern guards
`CleanupPostBorrowck` is the boundary that removes borrowck-only fake borrows from runtime MIR. The reusable rule is: every `Rvalue::Ref` whose kind is any `BorrowKind::Fake(_)` variant must be erased, including fake-borrow assignments and `FakeRead(ForMatchGuard, ...)` uses, and const/static promotion must reject every fake borrow rather than admitting it as an ordinary shared borrow. A shallow fake borrow protects only the immediately borrowed place, not projections, so a deref pattern whose guard can mutate through the scrutinee requires a deep fake borrow. A focused historical pair changes only the fake-borrow kind for the same place and guard: with the deep borrow, a guard write such as `*b = true` cannot alter the place used by later pattern tests; with the shallow borrow it can. This is a historical invariant; the supplied concrete pair does not by itself prove universal equivalence.
Preconditions: Borrowck has run and MIR contains `Rvalue::Ref(_, BorrowKind::Fake(FakeBorrowKind::Shallow|Deep), place)` from match-guard or deref-pattern lowering.; The fake-borrow enum has multiple variants, so matching only the old unit-like `BorrowKind::Fake` form is no longer sufficient.; `CleanupPostBorrowck` runs after borrowck and before runtime MIR consumers.; Promotion analysis for a const/static candidate body encounters a fake-borrowed place and must not promote it.; A deref pattern is used in a match with a guard that can mutate through the dereferenced scrutinee.
Pitfalls: Matching only the exact old `BorrowKind::Fake` after the enum gains a `FakeBorrowKind` payload is too narrow; cleanup and promotion must match `BorrowKind::Fake(_)`.; Do not treat deep fake borrows as ordinary promotable shared borrows merely because they look immutable or aliasable.; A shallow fake borrow only protects the borrowed place itself, not its projections; deref patterns need a deep fake borrow to block guard mutation such as `*b = true`.; The `SimplifyCfg` MIR-test hunks in the supplied patch mainly renumber locals and print `fake shallow`; they are not evidence of a separate `SimplifyCfg` semantic rewrite.; Do not infer that a passing run-pass test alone establishes the fake-borrow cleanup invariant; explicitly inspect the resulting MIR and the guard-mutation diagnostic.
Test strategy: Use a MIR test containing both `Fake(Shallow)` and `Fake(Deep)` borrows and assert that `CleanupPostBorrowck` replaces both fake-borrow assignments and `FakeRead(ForMatchGuard, ...)` uses with `nop`. Add UI coverage for deref patterns under guards; `tests/ui/pattern/deref-patterns/fake_borrows.rs` expects `E0510: cannot assign `*b` in match guard`. Keep run-pass deref-pattern tests for branch/bindings. For the differential, hold the source and place fixed and switch only `Fake(Shallow)` versus `Fake(Deep)`: with initial `false`, the correct deep version should still match `deref!(false)` and return 20, while the incorrect shallow version mutates the pointee, fails that match, and returns 30; the first `deref!(true)` arm returns 10 in both versions. Add a promotion-focused check that both fake-borrow kinds are rejected. A focused pair distinguishes the variants but is not universal proof.

### Downstream cleanup assertions should reflect early validated coverage SourceRegions and all-or-nothing MCDC decisions
`CleanupPostBorrowck` does not construct or repair coverage source regions; its coverage statements and dumps are downstream of `InstrumentCoverage`. The reusable invariant is that when coverage mappings are converted eagerly, each mapping span becomes a validated `SourceRegion` using the source map and HIR body span: empty spans are first expanded to an adjacent `{` or `}`, and a region is retained only when coordinates are nonzero, `end_col`'s high bit is clear, and start is not after end. For MCDC, a decision mapping is emitted only when every condition and the decision span convert successfully; otherwise conditions are downgraded to ordinary `MappingKind::Branch` mappings. Deferring conversion by storing raw `Span`s and filtering each mapping independently at codegen can leave zero-width/invalid regions or emit a decision while dropping individual condition mappings. Thus the same source program can produce different `InstrumentCoverage` output and different downstream `CleanupPostBorrowck` dumps, even though cleanup itself did not change. The supplied historical cases demonstrate concrete differences, not universal equivalence.
Preconditions: Coverage instrumentation is enabled for the function.; The function has coverage mappings, including at least one empty span or an MCDC decision with multiple condition mappings.; The mapping source file must be resolvable relative to the function body span; supplied coverage mappings are expected in the same file as the body span.; Source-region validation requires nonzero 1-based coordinates, an unset high bit in `end_col`, and ordered start/end positions.; The transformed behavior stores raw `Span`s and defers conversion/validation to LLVM coverage map generation, filtering mappings independently.
Pitfalls: Do not validate or filter MCDC conditions independently after the decision has already been queued. If any condition or the decision span is invalid, emit no `MCDCDecision`; downgrade all conditions to `MappingKind::Branch` instead.; Empty mapping spans must be expanded to an adjacent `{` or `}` before coordinate conversion; otherwise zero-width regions can appear, as shown by old expectations `14:10 - 14:10`, `21:2 - 21:2`, and `14:39 - 14:39`.; Incorrectly ordered or zero/misinterpreted coordinates can make `llvm-cov` fail; invalid regions should be dropped before codegen rather than relying on later filtering.; The deferred path's per-mapping skip can remove one condition while retaining the decision mapping, violating the MCDC decision/condition relationship.; After early `SourceRegion` construction, MIR dumps use numeric `line:col - line:col` formatting; after deferred raw `Span` storage, they may show path-bearing `Span` formatting such as `$DIR/file:line:col: line:col (#0)`.; `CleanupPostBorrowck` coverage metadata is inherited from instrumentation, so changes to source-region construction affect both instrumentation dumps and later cleanup expectations; do not 'fix' cleanup independently.

### A used coverage function can end with no valid regions when Span conversion is deferred; cleanup dumps should reflect Span-form mappings
When coverage mappings retain raw `Span`s through MIR and only convert to line/column coordinates during LLVM codegen, codegen-side validation can reject every span of a used function. The reusable correctness rule is that such a function is allowed to have zero remaining coverage regions: codegen must skip the covfun record gracefully (`return None`) instead of asserting or emitting zero-region metadata that `llvm-cov` may reject. `CleanupPostBorrowck` does not perform the deferred conversion; downstream dumps should simply show the mapping representation produced by instrumentation, such as `Span` text rather than numeric `SourceRegion` ranges. Empty spans may be expanded to an adjacent `{` or `}` within the function body span, but rejected spans are not emitted. The supplied testing-only flag and auxiliary test crate exercise the all-spans-discarded path; this is concrete historical evidence, not a claim that finite tests prove equivalence.

### Skip covfun records with unreferenced local file IDs; downstream cleanup shows spans enlarged earlier in instrumentation
LLVM requires every local file ID in a function's per-function coverage local file table to be referenced by at least one mapping region. The reusable rule is to scan all coverage spans and skip the entire covfun record if any local file ID is unseen, rather than deleting the unused table entry or emitting metadata that can trigger LLVM's assertion. Empty-span enlargement must happen before merge/dedup when it is moved into the InstrumentCoverage MIR pipeline; downstream `CleanupPostBorrowck` then carries the enlarged spans, so cleanup dumps change as a consequence of the upstream placement even though cleanup logic is unchanged. The supplied test-only `-Zcoverage-options=inject-unused-local-file` flag models an injected unused file entry, and empty spans adjacent to braces model the enlargement path. The distinction is important: zero regions after discarding is handled as a separate skip condition, while an unreferenced local file ID is a local-file-table consistency check.

### Treat empty-span enlargement location as an observable differential; CleanupPostBorrowck mirrors InstrumentCoverage rather than repairing spans
The supplied history contains both a re-land that moved empty-coverage-span enlargement out of LLVM codegen and into MIR InstrumentCoverage before merge (144298), and a revert that restored enlargement in codegen's `make_coords` (144480). The reusable testing rule is to treat the location of empty-span enlargement as a differential: hold the source and extracted spans fixed, vary only whether enlargement happens early in InstrumentCoverage before dedup/merge or late in codegen, and compare both the instrumentation output and the downstream `CleanupPostBorrowck` output. Early enlargement can make an empty span adjacent to a same-BCB neighbor and merge them, changing mapping counts; late enlargement leaves empty spans visible through InstrumentCoverage and `CleanupPostBorrowck`, with codegen widening them afterward. CleanupPostBorrowck should preserve whichever representation InstrumentCoverage emits; it should not independently enlarge or 'fix' empty spans. The historical revert shows that the early placement was sufficient to re-trigger prior failures, but the supplied cases are concrete evidence, not a claim that either direction is universally correct or that modern rustc is buggy.
Preconditions: Coverage instrumentation is enabled and InstrumentCoverage extracts/refines coverage spans.; At least one refined coverage span is empty (`span.lo() == span.hi()`).; Correct/late path: codegen map generation applies empty-span enlargement before computing coordinates.; Early path: InstrumentCoverage applies enlargement/discard with `retain_mut` before `dedup_by`, while codegen rejects empty spans.; Enlargement is possible only when the adjacent source byte is ASCII `{` after the span or ASCII `}` before it.
Pitfalls: Do not assume empty coverage spans are invalid during MIR instrumentation; under the late-enlargement ordering they are deliberately left empty until codegen coordinate generation.; Enlarging empty spans in InstrumentCoverage changes observable MIR coverage statements and is not equivalent to enlarging them in codegen, even if the eventual line/column widening can look similar.; `CleanupPostBorrowck` does not perform this enlargement; its empty-span expectations are downstream of InstrumentCoverage and must move with the instrumentation placement, not be fixed independently to non-empty spans.; The codegen `debug_assert!(false, "can't make coords from empty span")` belongs to the early-enlargement/reverted ordering; restoring `ensure_non_empty_span` in map generation avoids that path.; Changing `BytePos` by 1 is justified only after confirming the skipped byte is ASCII `{` or `}` at the appropriate adjacent position; `span_to_source` failure drops the empty span.; Early enlargement before merge can collapse mappings: the async-closure example changes from two file-0 mappings to one and raw bytes shrink from 14 to 9, so mapping-count expectations are part of the behavior.
Test strategy: Compare two builds that differ only in where empty-span enlargement happens, using the same coverage test source. Under the early placement, inspect `tests/mir-opt/coverage/*.InstrumentCoverage.diff` and the downstream `CleanupPostBorrowck` dump for widened coordinates such as `15:1: 15:2` and `21:1: 21:2`, and check `.cov-map` merging where `async_closure.cov-map` changes from two mappings to one and raw bytes from 14 to 9. Under the late placement/revert, assert that empty regions remain empty through InstrumentCoverage and `CleanupPostBorrowck`, e.g. `21:2: 21:2`, `29:2: 29:2`, `17:10: 17:10`, and `14:39: 14:39`, and that async-closure mapping splitting remains two mappings (`11:34` to `11:35` and `11:35` to `11:36`) rather than one widened mapping.
